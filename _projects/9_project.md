Expanding on social interactions and well-being, and considering the recent advancements in AI agents, here I’m exploring the impact of AI on supporting the social and emotional needs. AI-driven chatbots should offer non-judgmental, and accessible interactions, and facilitate social connections.

In the current study, I’m exploring the differences in empathy expression between human-human and human-AI interactions and investigating the factors that evoke empathy in both settings. Using GPT-4o and based on the best and worst personal stories, collected from a cohort of college students, our findings reveal that GPT-4 tends to overestimate empathy with less variability compared to humans, performing better in the affective dimension of empathy than in cognitive understanding. Fine-tuning significantly improves GPT-4's alignment with human empathy, however, the model still struggles with positive events and exhibits biases, such as overemphasizing empathy toward certain subgroups like women.
